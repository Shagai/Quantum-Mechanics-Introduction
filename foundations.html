The physical ideas before the full machinery.

This page builds the first mental model: amplitudes carry phase, paths interfere, qubits encode relative direction, measurement samples probabilities, and uncertainty limits jointly sharp descriptions.

Foundations 1. The state

Quantum states are not ordinary probability lists.

A quantum state stores amplitudes. An amplitude has a size and a phase. When a measurement is made, the size becomes an ordinary probability through the Born rule: \(P(\text{outcome})=|\text{amplitude}|^2\).

The phase is invisible if there is only one path. It matters when two or more paths can meet. Then amplitudes add before we square them, so phases can strengthen or cancel each other.

\[ \phi(p)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(x)e^{-ipx/\hbar}\,dx, \qquad \psi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(p)e^{ipx/\hbar}\,dp. \] \[ P(x)\,dx=|\psi(x)|^2dx,\qquad P(p)\,dp=|\phi(p)|^2dp,\qquad \langle p\rangle=\int \phi^*(p)p\phi(p)\,dp. \]

The uncertainty relation is a Fourier fact with physical consequences: a narrow packet needs many momentum components, while a nearly single-momentum state must spread across space.

\[ \hat p=-i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial x},\qquad \hat T(a)=e^{-ia\hat p/\hbar},\qquad (\hat T(a)\psi)(x)=\psi(x-a). \] \[ [\hat x,\hat p]\psi =\hat x(-i\hbar\partial_x\psi) +i\hbar\partial_x(x\psi) =i\hbar\psi. \]

This is why the canonical commutator is not arbitrary notation: it encodes how positions change under infinitesimal translations.

Foundations 2. The experiment

The double slit is phase bookkeeping made visible.

If a particle can reach a detector through two indistinguishable paths, the amplitudes from both paths are added first. Where phases line up, detection is likely. Where they oppose, detection is unlikely.

This is why the pattern is not the sum of two classical shadows. It is a probability distribution created by amplitude addition.

Foundations 3. The smallest example

A qubit is a point of direction, not a coin waiting to land.

A classical bit is either 0 or 1. A qubit can be in a superposition \(|\psi\rangle=\cos(\theta/2)|0\rangle+e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta/2)|1\rangle\). The angle theta controls the measurement probabilities in the 0 and 1 basis. The angle phi is a relative phase that becomes visible in other measurement bases.

Foundations 4. The update

Measurement turns amplitude into sampled fact.

Quantum mechanics predicts probabilities for outcomes, not a hidden list of prewritten answers. Repeating the same preparation many times reveals the probability distribution.

\[ \hat P_a=\sum_{\mu}|a,\mu\rangle\langle a,\mu|,\qquad p(a)=\langle\psi|\hat P_a|\psi\rangle. \] \[ |\psi\rangle\longrightarrow \frac{\hat P_a|\psi\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle\psi|\hat P_a|\psi\rangle}}, \qquad \hat P_a\hat P_b=\delta_{ab}\hat P_a,\quad \sum_a\hat P_a=I. \]

The probability is the length squared of the projected component. For a degenerate eigenspace, the relative amplitudes inside that subspace survive the measurement.

Foundations 5. The constraint

Uncertainty is a limit on jointly sharp descriptions.

A localized wave packet is built from many momentum components. A broad packet can be made from a narrower range of momenta. That tradeoff is the content of the uncertainty principle: \( \Delta x\,\Delta p \ge \hbar/2 \).

\[ \Delta A=\sqrt{\langle A^2\rangle-\langle A\rangle^2},\qquad \Delta B=\sqrt{\langle B^2\rangle-\langle B\rangle^2}. \] \[ \Delta A\,\Delta B\geq \frac{1}{2}\left|\langle[\hat A,\hat B]\rangle\right|. \] \[ A=\sigma_z,\qquad B(\alpha)=\cos\alpha\,\sigma_z+\sin\alpha\,\sigma_x,\qquad \frac{1}{2}|\langle[A,B]\rangle|=|\sin\alpha\,\langle\sigma_y\rangle|. \]

The inequality is a statement about a prepared state, not about bad instruments. It comes from Cauchy-Schwarz applied to the vectors \((A-\langle A\rangle)|\psi\rangle\) and \((B-\langle B\rangle)|\psi\rangle\).

\[ \rho(x,t)=|\Psi(x,t)|^2,\qquad j(x,t)=\frac{\hbar}{m}\operatorname{Im}\!\left(\Psi^*\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial x}\right). \] \[ \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial j}{\partial x}=0,\qquad \frac{d}{dt}\int_a^b \rho(x,t)\,dx=j(a,t)-j(b,t). \]

This is the local version of normalization. The Schrödinger equation does not merely preserve total probability; it says where probability flows.
