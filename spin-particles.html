Angular momentum and exchange symmetry change what states are allowed.

This page moves from orbital angular momentum to intrinsic spin, spin coupling, and the symmetry rule that separates bosons from fermions.

Spin 1. Angular momentum and spin

Spin is intrinsic angular momentum with discrete projections.

Orbital angular momentum comes from rotations in space. Spin obeys the same angular-momentum algebra, but it is not a tiny classical rotation of a material object. A spin-\(\tfrac12\) state has only two possible \(S_z\) measurement results: \(m_s=+\tfrac12\) or \(m_s=-\tfrac12\).

The commutator algebra fixes the ladder structure. Once \(j\) is known, the allowed projections are \(m=-j,-j+1,\ldots,j\), and the operators \(\hat J_\pm\) move between neighboring rungs.

Angular momentum algebra

\[ [\hat J_i,\hat J_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat J_k, \qquad \hat J^2|j,m\rangle=\hbar^2j(j+1)|j,m\rangle. \]

Ladder operators

\[ \hat J_\pm|j,m\rangle =\hbar\sqrt{j(j+1)-m(m\pm1)}\,|j,m\pm1\rangle. \] The ladder stops because the square-root factor becomes zero at \(m=\pm j\).

Spin-\(\tfrac12\) measurement

\[ |\chi\rangle=\cos\frac{\beta}{2}|+\rangle +e^{i\alpha}\sin\frac{\beta}{2}|-\rangle, \qquad P(+z)=\cos^2\frac{\beta}{2}. \]

\[ |1,1\rangle=|+\rangle|+\rangle,\qquad |1,-1\rangle=|-\rangle|-\rangle. \] \[ |1,0\rangle=\frac{|+\rangle|-\rangle+|-\rangle|+\rangle}{\sqrt2}. \] \[ |0,0\rangle=\frac{|+\rangle|-\rangle-|-\rangle|+\rangle}{\sqrt2}. \] \[ |\psi\rangle=\cos\gamma\,|+\rangle|-\rangle +e^{i\phi}\sin\gamma\,|-\rangle|+\rangle. \] \[ P_{1,0}=\frac{1+\sin(2\gamma)\cos\phi}{2}. \] \[ P_{0,0}=\frac{1-\sin(2\gamma)\cos\phi}{2}. \]

Clebsch-Gordan coefficients are amplitudes for changing from uncoupled labels \((m_1,m_2)\) to total labels \((j,m)\).

Spin 2. Identical particles

Exchange symmetry changes the shape of two-particle probability.

Identical quantum particles are not merely hard to label. The physical state must transform in a definite way when the particle labels are exchanged: \(\hat P_{12}\Psi=\pm\Psi\). The plus sign describes bosonic symmetry; the minus sign describes fermionic antisymmetry.

For two one-particle orbitals \(\psi_a\) and \(\psi_b\), the allowed two-particle spatial states are symmetric or antisymmetric combinations. This is the mathematical source of bunching, antibunching, and the Pauli exclusion principle.

Symmetric and antisymmetric states

\[ \Psi_\pm(x_1,x_2)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(1\pm |S|^2)}} \left[\psi_a(x_1)\psi_b(x_2)\pm\psi_b(x_1)\psi_a(x_2)\right]. \]

Exchange eigenvalue

\[ \hat P_{12}\Psi_\pm(x_1,x_2)=\Psi_\pm(x_2,x_1)=\pm\Psi_\pm(x_1,x_2). \] Observables depend on \(|\Psi|^2\), but the sign controls interference between exchanged alternatives.

Pauli exclusion

If two fermions have the same spin state, their spatial state must be antisymmetric. Then \[ \Psi_-(x,x)=0, \] so two identical fermions cannot occupy the same complete one-particle state.
