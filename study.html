Study 1. Chapter-level study guide

Each topic should end with a derivation you can reproduce.

A deep quantum mechanics course is learned by rebuilding the machinery yourself. The guide below is the current project backlog for turning this article into a full study text: every chapter has a conceptual target, a mathematical derivation target, and a problem ladder.

01 Wave mechanics and probability From \(\Psi\) to measurable distributions

Build the interpretation of \(|\Psi(x,t)|^2\), normalization, expectation values, and the continuity equation: \[ \frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial j}{\partial x}=0 \] \[ j=\frac{\hbar}{2mi}\left(\Psi^*\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial x} -\Psi\frac{\partial\Psi^*}{\partial x}\right). \]

  • Derive conservation of total probability from the Schrodinger equation.
  • Compute \(\langle x\rangle\), \(\langle p\rangle\), \(\Delta x\), and \(\Delta p\) for a Gaussian.
  • Explain why global phase is unobservable but relative phase is physical.
02 Stationary states and one-dimensional wells Boundary conditions create spectra

Separate variables with \(\Psi(x,t)=\psi(x)e^{-iEt/\hbar}\), solve \(\hat H\psi=E\psi\), and connect allowed energies to normalizable eigenfunctions.

  • Derive the infinite-well spectrum and prove orthonormality.
  • Match \(\psi\) and \(d\psi/dx\) across finite potential steps.
  • Compare bound states, continuum states, and resonance-like behavior.
03 Operators, Hilbert space, and uncertainty The algebra behind measurement

Translate wave mechanics into vector spaces. Observables become Hermitian operators, and incompatible observables satisfy nonzero commutators: \[ \Delta A\,\Delta B \ge \frac12|\langle[\hat A,\hat B]\rangle|. \]

  • Show that Hermitian operators have real eigenvalues.
  • Derive \([\hat x,\hat p]=i\hbar\) and the Heisenberg uncertainty relation.
  • Use basis changes to compute probabilities in a non-position basis.
04 Harmonic oscillator The first algebraic solution

Factor the Hamiltonian into ladder operators: \[ \hat a=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x+\frac{i}{m\omega}\hat p\right), \qquad \hat H=\hbar\omega\left(\hat a^\dagger\hat a+\frac12\right). \]

  • Prove that energy spacing is uniform and the ground energy is not zero.
  • Construct \(\psi_0(x)\) from \(\hat a\psi_0=0\).
  • Connect coherent states to classical oscillation.
05 Three dimensions and hydrogen Symmetry separates the problem

In a central potential, separation in spherical coordinates produces radial functions and spherical harmonics: \[ \Psi(r,\theta,\phi)=R_{n\ell}(r)Y_\ell^m(\theta,\phi). \]

  • Identify degeneracy from rotational symmetry.
  • Interpret quantum numbers \(n\), \(\ell\), and \(m\).
  • Sketch radial probability \(r^2|R_{n\ell}(r)|^2\) for low-lying states.
06 Spin, angular momentum, and addition Discrete directions from noncommuting generators

Angular momentum is governed by \[ [\hat J_i,\hat J_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat J_k, \qquad \hat J_\pm|j,m\rangle=\hbar\sqrt{j(j+1)-m(m\pm1)}|j,m\pm1\rangle. \]

  • Derive allowed \(m\) values using ladder operators.
  • Represent spin-\(\tfrac12\) states with Pauli matrices.
  • Use Clebsch-Gordan logic to combine two spins.
07 Approximation methods How real systems become calculable

Perturbation theory, the variational principle, and WKB each preserve a different structure: algebraic corrections, energy bounds, and semiclassical phase.

  • Compute first-order corrections for a weak anharmonic perturbation.
  • Diagonalize a two-state perturbation matrix when unperturbed states are degenerate.
  • Use a variational Gaussian to estimate a ground-state energy.
  • Apply WKB quantization \(\int_{x_1}^{x_2}p(x)\,dx=(n+\tfrac12)\pi\hbar\).
08 Scattering, identical particles, and transitions From bound spectra to experiments

Scattering amplitudes, exchange symmetry, and time-dependent perturbations connect the formal machinery to cross sections, atomic structure, and transition rates: \[ \Gamma_{i\to f}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}|\langle f|\hat H'|i\rangle|^2\rho(E_f). \]

  • Relate \(f(\theta)\) to \(d\sigma/d\Omega\).
  • Compute \(\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}\) from partial-wave phase shifts.
  • Build symmetric and antisymmetric two-particle states.
  • Use selection rules to decide which transitions are allowed.

Study 2. Study path

A deep pass means reading, manipulating, and deriving.

Use the article as a spiral rather than a straight line. Each pass should end with a calculation you can reproduce and a figure whose limiting cases you can predict before touching the slider.

01

Amplitudes before formalism

Read amplitudes, interference, qubits, and measurement. Derive \(P(k)=|\langle k|\psi\rangle|^2\).

02

Postulates as working rules

Practice expectation values, projectors, commutators, and \(\Delta A\,\Delta B\) bounds.

03

Exact systems

Move from wells and tunneling to oscillator ladders, hydrogen separation, and spherical harmonics.

04

Spin and exchange

Use angular momentum algebra, spin coupling, and \(\hat P_{12}\Psi=\pm\Psi\) to explain exclusion.

05

Approximation methods

Compare perturbation theory, degenerate mixing, variational bounds, Stark splitting, and WKB.

06

Scattering and transitions

Connect \(d\sigma/d\Omega=|f(\theta)|^2\), Fermi's golden rule, and selection rules to experiments.

Study 3. Practice problems

The fastest check is whether you can derive the next line.

The full problem set lives in PROBLEM_SET.md. These six representative prompts match the study passes above and are designed to be solved with the nearby interactive figures open.

Interference term

For \( |\psi\rangle=(|a\rangle+e^{i\phi}|b\rangle)/\sqrt2 \), derive \(P(x)=|A_a(x)+e^{i\phi}A_b(x)|^2/2\) and identify the phase-dependent term.

Projection update

Given \(P_a+P_b=I\), show why outcome \(a\) sends \( |\psi\rangle \) to \(P_a|\psi\rangle/\sqrt{\langle\psi|P_a|\psi\rangle}\).

Stationary states

Solve the infinite square well on \(0\lt x\lt L\), normalize the eigenfunctions, and prove orthogonality for two different quantum numbers.

Spin and exchange

Build symmetric and antisymmetric two-particle states from \(\phi_a(x_1)\phi_b(x_2)\) and \(\phi_b(x_1)\phi_a(x_2)\), then evaluate the case \(a=b\).

Approximation test

Starting from \(\hat H=\hat H_0+\lambda\hat H'\), derive \(E_n^{(1)}=\langle n^{(0)}|\hat H'|n^{(0)}\rangle\) for a nondegenerate level.

Transition rate

Explain why Fermi's golden rule contains both \(|\langle f|\hat H'|i\rangle|^2\) and a density of final states \(\rho(E_f)\).
